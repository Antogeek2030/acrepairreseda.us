Summer in the San Fernando Valley arrives early and stays late. Preparing your air conditioner before the first real heat wave can mean the difference between steady comfort and a stressful emergency repair.

## Start with a Visual Inspection

Walk around the outdoor condenser unit. Clear away leaves, grass clippings, and any debris that has accumulated over the cooler months. Make sure there is at least two feet of clear space on all sides so air can flow freely. Check that the unit sits level; a tilted condenser can strain the compressor.

Indoors, look at the air filter. If it looks dirty or has been in place more than three months, replace it. A clogged filter forces the system to work harder and reduces cooling capacity.

## Test the System Early

Turn the thermostat to cooling mode on a mild day and listen. Unusual noises—rattling, grinding, or squealing—deserve professional attention before temperatures climb. Confirm that cool air is coming from the vents within a few minutes and that the outdoor unit is running smoothly.

## Consider a Professional Tune-Up

A seasonal tune-up typically includes cleaning the coils, checking refrigerant levels, testing electrical connections, lubricating moving parts, and verifying thermostat calibration. These steps improve efficiency and often uncover small issues before they become expensive failures.

## Programmable or Smart Thermostats Help

If you still have a basic thermostat, upgrading to a programmable or smart model lets you raise the temperature while the house is empty and cool it before you return. Many models also send maintenance reminders and alert you to problems.

## When to Call for Help

If the system struggles to reach the set temperature, cycles on and off frequently, or produces warm air, schedule a service visit promptly. Early diagnosis usually costs less than waiting until the unit fails on a 100-degree afternoon.
